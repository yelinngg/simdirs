"Free eSIM" means at least four different things in 2026, and mixing them up is how people end up disappointed at the airport. This guide catalogs every genuinely free offer we could verify from official pages — new-user trials, ad-supported data, bundled perks and long-validity free claims — and states the catch that comes with each. Numbers below were checked against provider pages in October 2026; promos change, so treat this as a map, not a contract.

## The four kinds of "free"

- **A free profile with paid data.** The eSIM itself costs nothing to issue; you pay for data. Almost every provider works this way — not really an offer, just the baseline.
- **A new-user trial.** A small block of data for $0, once per person, to test the network before buying. This is the most common real offer.
- **An ad-supported tier.** Watch ads, get data. Genuinely recurring free data, but speed-capped.
- **A bundled perk.** Free eSIM data attached to something else you hold — a Visa card, a payment app, a subscription.

## The verified offers

### co:sim — 1GB / 7 days, Europe 33 countries

The most generous straightforward trial we found. New users claim a [free 1GB / 7-day eSIM](https://cosim.io/en/free-esim) that works as one bundle across 33 European countries, with separate free allocations for Japan, Hong Kong, South Korea, Thailand, Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Turkey and Moldova. The QR code arrives by email; top-ups are available anytime.

The catch: **card verification is required even at $0 checkout**, and it is new users only. The operator (Comsand Inc., Delaware) is young — use the trial, don't build your digital life on it. Our [co:sim listing](/sims/cosim) keeps the current terms.

### dtcpay eSIM — free claim, 10-year validity

The odd one out. [dtcpay](/sims/dtcpay-esim) is a MAS-licensed Singapore payment app (PS20200531) whose eSIM line is officially promoted with **10-year validity, no throttling, reuse across devices** across 158+ countries. Community guides report a recurring in-app promo: a $10 eSIM claimed at USD 0, occasionally stackable, valid toward 2036.

The catch: the promo's terms are **not published on official pages** — it lives in the app and can change anytime. Claiming requires a dtcpay account and KYC. It is data-only: no number, no calls, no SMS. Mainland-China iPhones may not support eSIM at all.

### Nomad — 1GB / 3 days, 76 destinations

A clean, well-documented trial: [1GB free for 3 days](https://www.nomadesim.com/documents/landing-trial-plan) in 76 destinations, exclusive to new users, no credit card required, hotspot supported. If you want a zero-friction test eSIM before a short trip, this is the least conditional offer in the list.

The catch: 3 days and 1GB is exactly enough to evaluate, not to travel on.

### Firsty — ad-supported, up to 360MB/day

Not a trial but a **permanent free tier**: watch a 15-30 second ad, get 20MB at 1 Mbps, stackable up to 18 ads / 360MB per day, data valid 7 days, max 240MB held at once. Firsty's own help centre documents all four limits, which is rarer than it should be. Coverage spans roughly 170 countries.

The catch: 1 Mbps is messaging-and-maps speed, no hotspot, and the June 2026 terms also cap each ad-unlock at a 30-minute session (6 hours total). Hitting the daily ceiling takes about 18 ads — real time spent for ~360MB. Our [Firsty listing](/sims/firsty) tracks the current limits.

### Eskimo — 250MB global trial, never-expiring data

[Eskimo](/sims/eskimo)'s angle is longevity: paid data never expires, and the free tier is a 250MB global trial with no deadline pressure. Good as a "keep an eSIM installed for emergencies" card — install it, forget it, and the data is still there months later.

The catch: 250MB is a taster; the value shows up only if you later buy their non-expiring packs.

### GigSky — up to 100MB trial, up to 5GB for Visa cardholders

GigSky runs a [standard free trial](https://www.gigsky.com/free-offering) of up to 100MB over 7 days in 125+ destinations (plus a 100MB cruise variant for 280+ ships), no card required. The bigger prize is the **Visa perk: up to 5GB free for eligible Visa cardholders** — one of the largest legitimately free blocks available, if your card qualifies.

The catch: the 5GB depends on your Visa issuing bank opting in; check the eligibility page before assuming yours is covered.

### USIMS — 1GB free plus permanent low-speed floor

[USIMS](/sims/usims) gives new users 1GB in the US/EU, and — unusually — keeps a **permanent low-speed data floor alive in 200+ countries** after the fast data is gone. For a "never fully offline" emergency line, that floor is the real feature.

The catch: app-only, and community reports note it blocks China-market iPhones and 5ber/eSTK adapters — check device requirements before counting on it.

### Roamless — 500MB free on sign-up

[Roamless](/sims/roamless) includes 500MB free with a pay-as-you-go structure afterward. Simple claim, no trial window pressure.

## Comparison at a glance

- **Biggest one-shot**: GigSky Visa perk (up to 5GB, card-dependent)
- **Biggest no-strings trial**: co:sim 1GB/7d (EU 33 countries) or Nomad 1GB/3d (76 destinations)
- **Recurring free**: Firsty ad tier (360MB/day ceiling)
- **Longest shelf life**: dtcpay 10-year free claim (promo terms app-only) and Eskimo never-expiring
- **Emergency floor**: USIMS permanent low-speed in 200+ countries

## What "free" costs you anyway

Every offer above has a non-monetary price. Trials want your email and a card verification; ad tiers want your attention (about two hours of ads for 5GB on a two-week trip, by Firsty's own numbers); payment-app eSIMs want a full KYC account; cardholder perks want the right card in your wallet. None of that is a scam — it is the business model — but read which one you are paying with before you claim.

Two structural notes from our catalog work: first, free-tier eSIMs are **data-only lines** — no number, no SMS, so they do not replace a keep-alive SIM if you need to receive codes (see our [UK number keep-alive guide](/news/uk-sim-keep-alive-guide-2026/) for that job). Second, the exit IP of a free eSIM is often a datacenter in a third country — if you are registering accounts, check what IP you actually present ([our IP exit guide](/news/esim-exit-ip-guide-2026/) covers how).

Free trials also change without notice: several providers have adjusted amounts, validity and eligibility during 2026. We re-verify the listings linked above periodically; the provider's own page at claim time is the final word.
