Japan is one of the most competitive travel eSIM markets in the world: excellent networks, aggressively low prices. But more choice means more traps — the word "unlimited" hides three different products, from full-speed KDDI lines to bundles that throttle to 256 kbps. Every price below was verified from official channels in August 2026.

## The three kinds of "unlimited" in Japan

Before buying, learn to tell these apart:

1. **True unlimited**: full speed, no cap, riding tier-1 carriers like KDDI. BCeSIM's KDDI 5G line is the reference: $10.70 for 4 days, $17.60 for 7 days, $33.80 for 15 days, $71.20 for 31 days — hotspot explicitly supported.
2. **Daily-refresh unlimited**: a high-speed allowance each day that throttles (never cuts) when exhausted, resetting every 24 hours. RedteaGO basic at $0.70/day for 300 MB/day, premium at $3.90/day for 10 GB/day, with 1–180 day flexibility.
3. **Bucket with throttled overrun**: fixed data that switches to slow-but-working speed after the cap. BCeSIM's IIJ line is typical: $3.20 for 3 GB up to $18.50 for 30 GB, throttling to 256 kbps afterwards.

The practical gap is huge: true unlimited streams 4K without blinking; daily-refresh suits heavy daytime use with overnight recharge; throttled overrun leaves you messaging-and-maps only once spent.

## Match the plan to your trip

**3–5 day city break (Tokyo/Osaka)**: under 1 GB/day, RedteaGO's 1 GB at $2.50 or 3 GB at $6.90; BCeSIM's IIJ 3 GB at $3.20 is sharper still. Budget $3–7 total. For zero effort, BCeSIM's KDDI 4-day true unlimited at $10.70.

**7–10 day standard trip**: moderate use (1.5–2 GB/day) puts three routes within pennies of each other: Airalo ~$13.50 for 10 GB (30-day validity), RedteaGO $9.50 for 10 GB, or Holafly unlimited for the same duration. At this level you're comparing network quality, not unit price — single-network packs can get congested in Tokyo's rush hour.

**14+ days / heavy use**: go unlimited. BCeSIM KDDI at $33.80 for 15 days (~$2.25/day) is the benchmark full-speed price; RedteaGO premium's 10 GB/day refresh at $3.90/day wins above ~5 GB/day average; for month-long stays, KDDI at $71.20 for 31 days (~$2.30/day) is nearly unbeatable.

**Uncertain usage / safety net**: RedteaGO basic at $0.70/day (300 MB/day, throttled but never off) is the lowest barrier; Drimsim's per-MB billing suits messaging-and-maps minimalists.

## Network quality: KDDI, NTT docomo or IIJ?

The real-world experience depends heavily on which network you land on:

- **KDDI (au)**: the premium route for travel eSIMs — balanced urban/rural performance, aggressive 5G rollout. BCeSIM's unlimited line rides it.
- **NTT docomo**: the widest coverage, strongest in rural and mountain areas; most mainstream platforms default to it or can switch.
- **IIJ**: an MVNO route with the lowest prices, but speeds squeeze at peak, and overrun throttling sits at 256 kbps.

Simple rule: city-only itineraries can take the cheapest; for mountain routes (Alps, Nikko, Shirakawa-go) prefer docomo or KDDI — the network label is printed on every purchase page.

## Practical buying notes

- Most Japan eSIMs activate on first connection in-country, so buying and installing before departure doesn't burn validity; BCeSIM's carrier validity runs 60–180 days, making it stockpile-friendly.
- Hotspot: the KDDI unlimited line supports it explicitly; some budget packs cap or disable sharing — the purchase page says so.
- Device bands: Japan's 5G is Sub-6, fully compatible with recent iPhone/Android flagships; US mmWave models simply won't find mmWave here, but Sub-6 works normally.
