eSIMs have made staying connected abroad dramatically easier, but we still see the same mistakes trip after trip. Here are the seven most common ones — and how to dodge them.

## 1. Buying at the airport

Airport Wi-Fi is slow and prices at physical counters are inflated. Buy and install your eSIM a day or two before departure, while you still have reliable internet.

There is also a troubleshooting margin to consider: if installation hits a device-compatibility snag, an expired QR code, or a support ticket, fixing it at home beats fixing it 40 minutes before boarding. Queuing at a arrivals-hall counter for a physical SIM averages 20+ minutes; a pre-installed eSIM connects the moment you land.

**Do this:** treat the eSIM purchase as part of packing — buy and install within 48 hours of departure, and do one dry-run install on your home Wi-Fi.

## 2. Installing but not activating

Most travel eSIMs only activate when they first connect to a supported network, but a few start counting down immediately. Check the activation policy before you fly.

Each policy suits a different strategy: activate-on-arrival plans are safe to buy weeks ahead; activate-on-purchase plans need timing, or your clock starts running before you leave. Provider pages spell it out as "validity starts from first use" versus "activation on installation" — read that line before checking out.

**Do this:** find the activation-rule wording in the plan terms before buying; if it activates on installation, install it the night before you fly.

## 3. Forgetting the QR code

Keep your QR code or app account details backed up. If you delete the eSIM profile by accident, you will need it to reinstall.

A deleted eSIM profile cannot be recovered from the phone — only re-downloaded via the original QR code or your provider account. Do both: screenshot the QR code from your confirmation email (into a locked photo folder), and register a provider-app account instead of buying as a guest — the account keeps reinstall links for every past order.

**Do this:** within one minute of purchase — screenshot the QR code, bind the order to an account, then travel normally.

## 4. Ignoring device compatibility

Not every phone supports eSIM. Check your device settings before purchasing, or pick a provider that also ships physical SIM cards — see the directory filter for options.

iPhones from the XS/XR generation onward, recent Google Pixels and most flagship Androids support eSIM — but mid-tier models in the same lineups often drop the feature, and dual-SIM phones sometimes support it on only one slot. Check: on iOS, Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM; on Android, Settings → Network → SIM manager. Seeing the Add eSIM entry means support. Also watch for carrier locks: subsidized/carrier-locked phones may refuse third-party eSIM profiles entirely.

**Do this:** spend 30 seconds confirming the Add eSIM entry exists in Settings and the device is carrier-unlocked, before paying.

## 5. Overlooking regional plans

If your trip spans several neighboring countries, a regional plan is usually much cheaper than a stack of single-country packages.

In Europe, buying separate 5 GB local plans for France, Germany and Italy usually costs more than one regional plan covering 30+ countries; in Asia, a regional pack spanning Japan, Korea and ten Southeast Asian countries often lands 30–50% lower per GB than single-country packs. The trade-off: regional plans may ride slightly lower-priority networks than local plans, and data is a shared pool across all covered countries.

**Do this:** once your itinerary is fixed, check the regional plan's country list first — only buy single-country packs when every stop is covered and the per-GB price clearly wins.

## 6. Disabling data roaming

Travel eSIMs need data roaming switched ON to work. It sounds scary, but roaming charges only apply to your home SIM — turn that one off instead.

The correct configuration: home SIM (your domestic carrier) with **roaming OFF, data OFF**; eSIM line with **roaming ON, data ON**. Your home SIM then incurs zero data charges while the eSIM connects normally. On iOS, also check "Cellular Data Switching" isn't pointed at the wrong line; on Android, verify "Default data line" points to the eSIM.

**Do this:** after landing, toggle airplane mode on and off, wait ~30 seconds for network selection, and confirm the status-bar signal belongs to the eSIM line before using data.

## 7. Skipping coverage checks

Coverage and speed vary by country even within the same provider. Browse our [SIM directory](/sims) to compare coverage before committing.

The same eSIM brand may ride premium KDDI routing in Japan but a single partner carrier in a small country; strong urban signal says nothing about mountain roads. Also decode what "unlimited" really means: some plans are full-speed unlimited, others throttle after a high-speed allowance — usually documented as a fair use policy. The gap between the two is significant in practice.

**Do this:** compare destination coverage and partner networks in the directory; for "unlimited" plans, confirm the high-speed allowance and throttle threshold first.

## Bonus: the 10-minute pre-departure checklist

- QR code screenshotted, order bound to an account
- "Add eSIM" entry confirmed in Settings
- Home SIM data/roaming OFF, eSIM roaming ON
- Activate-on-arrival plan installed and waiting
- Provider support entry (in-app or web) bookmarked

Run through this list and almost every eSIM failure mode is eliminated before the airport. If you haven't picked a provider yet, filter and compare by destination and budget in the [SIM directory](/sims).
